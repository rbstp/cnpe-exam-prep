At first glance this competency sounds like management slides, but it turns into very concrete exam work: "write a query showing deployment frequency" is completely gradable. The key is knowing which real cluster metrics map to which delivery and platform indicators.

needsmake core obs

Orientation

competency 4.2 · measuring and improving platform efficiency

In practice, measuring platform efficiency means wiring your delivery controllers into Prometheus and querying the signals they already emit. Getting the scrape hooked up is half the job; the PromQL itself is just a few lines.

Two distinct metric families: don't blur them

Delivery performance (the DORA metrics) tells you how smoothly code reaches production. Platform effectiveness (fulfillment latency, adoption, time to first change, developer satisfaction) tells you how well the platform actually serves its users. A platform can deploy fast and still feel painful to use, and vice versa, which is why the CNCF Platforms white paper tracks both.

In an Argo CD shop, the controller already holds your delivery metrics

  • argocd_app_sync_total: per-app counter labeled by phase (Succeeded, Failed, Error); gives you deployment frequency and change failure rate directly.
  • argocd_app_info: one series per Application carrying current sync_status and health_status labels, perfect for "how many apps are degraded right now?"
  • argocd_app_reconcile (histogram): controller loop duration, measuring platform health rather than app delivery.

Argo CD already exports all three, but Prometheus doesn't scrape them until you drop in a ServiceMonitor, which is exactly how the exercises begin.

The other half: does the platform keep what it shipped running?

Pair delivery speed with runtime stability: restart counts (kube_pod_container_status_restarts_total), unavailable replicas (kube_deployment_status_replicas_unavailable), Pending pods, and the request-vs-usage efficiency gap from section 1.5. Delivery metrics prove you ship fast; runtime metrics prove what you ship actually holds up.

Watch the nuance when counting syncs

Counting Argo CD syncs as deployment frequency is a rough proxy: a self-heal sync on an unchanged app isn't a new release, and one commit fanning out across ten apps isn't ten independent releases. Call out that caveat clearly and mention what makes it exact (counting distinct Git revisions or emitting explicit deploy events from CI).

Modern DORA definitions and SPACE

the 2024 vocabulary, the retired tiers, what the white paper actually lists

DORA updated its metric names and groupings in 2024, so don't get tripped up when a prompt mentions "failed deployment recovery time" or "deployment rework rate":

GroupMetric (2024 name)Older nameDefinition
ThroughputChange lead timeLead time for changescommit to running in production
ThroughputDeployment frequencysamehow often you deploy to production
ThroughputFailed deployment recovery timeTime to restore service / MTTRrecovery time after a bad deployment (distinct from general infrastructure outages)
StabilityChange failure ratesameshare of deployments requiring remediation
StabilityDeployment rework rate (added 2024)noneshare of deployments that are unplanned hotfixes for user-facing bugs

Operational reliability sits beside these rather than acting as a "fifth key." For reference, the 2024 report's clusters were: Elite (on-demand deploys, lead time < 1 day, recovery < 1 hour, ~5% CFR), High (daily–weekly, 1 day–1 week, < 1 day, ~20%), Medium (weekly–monthly, 1 week–1 month, < 1 day, ~10%), and Low (monthly–biannual, 1–6 months, 1 week–1 month, ~40%). Note that the 2025 DORA report retired those four tiers in favor of team archetypes factoring in burnout and friction, so cite them as the 2024 clusters rather than fixed law.

Pulling each DORA metric out of a Kubernetes cluster

  • Deployment frequency: increase(argocd_app_sync_total{phase="Succeeded"}[7d]) per app; for Flux, count changes in the revision label on gotk_resource_info{customresource_kind="Kustomization"}; for Tekton, increase(tekton_pipelines_controller_pipelinerun_total{status="success"}[7d]) on your deploy pipeline.
  • Change lead time: requires joining two timestamps: the Git commit time (git log --format=%cI <sha>) and the deploy timestamp (from argocd app history or Flux Kustomization's status.lastAppliedRevision / condition lastTransitionTime). Because no single exporter owns both, a tiny pipeline step or controller usually joins them and emits a gauge.
  • Change failure rate: failed syncs (phase=~"Failed|Error") plus rollbacks and aborted progressive rollouts (rollout_phase{phase="Degraded"} in Argo Rollouts, or Flagger's flagger_canary_status failures) divided by total deploys.
  • Failed deployment recovery time: time from a deploy-triggered alert firing (ALERTS{alertstate="firing"}) to resolving, or from a broken sync to the next green sync (alongside MTTD, MTTA, and MTTR from your incident tooling).
  • Rework rate: relies on tagging unplanned hotfixes consistently (a commit trailer, PR label, or reason=hotfix annotation) so you can count them cleanly.

SPACE (why the white paper cites it)

When measuring developer productivity, the white paper points to SPACE: Satisfaction and well-being, Performance (outcomes, not raw output), Activity, Communication and collaboration, and Efficiency and flow. Its core rule: always combine metrics from at least three dimensions, including at least one perceptual measure like a developer survey. Judging teams by lines of code alone is pure Activity, which is exactly what SPACE warns against.

Measuring the platform itself

the white paper's list, maturity-model levels, SLIs for a platform API

The CNCF Platforms white paper organizes platform success into three buckets. Know these exact terms, because scenario marking keys look for them:

GroupMeasures namedWhere the data lives
User satisfaction and productivityactive users and retention (capabilities provisioned, growth/churn), NPS or developer surveys, SPACE metricsportal analytics, service catalog, surveys
Organizational efficiencyrequest-to-fulfillment latency for a capability, time to build and deploy a brand-new service to prod, time for a new engineer to ship their first changeCR timestamps (creation → Ready), scaffolder runs to first green sync, onboarding logs
Product and feature deliverythe DORA four: deployment frequency, lead time, time to restore, change failure rateCD and alerting systems

In the Platform Engineering Maturity Model, the Measurement track climbs from Ad hoc → Consistent collection → Insights → Quantitative and qualitative. Putting up a dashboard is Level 2; noticing request-to-Ready p95 is 20 minutes and cutting it to 2 minutes is Level 3.

Concrete platform KPIs you can compute right now

  • Adoption: count Crossplane XRs or kro instances per team (count by (customresource_kind) (kube_customresource_info) via kube-state-metrics CustomResourceStateMetrics), and compare platform-stamped namespaces against hand-rolled ones.
  • Golden path usage: compare Applications spawned by your ApplicationSet against manually created Applications, or track weekly Backstage scaffolder runs.
  • Request-to-fulfillment latency: subtract creationTimestamp from the Ready condition's lastTransitionTime (via CustomResourceStateMetrics or Crossplane's built-in crossplane_managed_resource_first_time_to_readiness_seconds histogram).
  • Ticket volume: track manual platform requests that self-service should be absorbing; watch this curve trend down.
  • Platform reliability: set real SLIs and SLOs for your own control plane (API server 5xx ratio, Argo CD sync success rate, webhook admission latency (apiserver_admission_webhook_admission_duration_seconds), and Crossplane/kro reconcile errors (controller_runtime_reconcile_total{result="error"})), guarded by the exact same multi-window burn-rate alerts from section 4.2.
How this gets tested

Expect two flavors of question: hands-on ("write a PromQL query for deployment frequency, failed syncs, or unhealthy apps" after scraping the tool), and architectural ("which indicators prove your platform improved developer experience?": cite fulfillment latency, time to first change, adoption, and surveys alongside DORA, and explain why raw output counts like lines of code don't work).

Self-check

answer before opening
Name the DORA four and the system that holds each one's raw data in this lab.

Deployment frequency: Argo CD sync counters. Lead time: Git commit timestamps joined with CD deploy timestamps. Change failure rate: sync phases and rollout outcomes. Time to restore: alert history (firing to resolved) or incident records. None of them live in one single tool, which is why wiring telemetry together matters.

Why increase() rather than the raw counter for deployment frequency?

A raw counter climbs forever from startup and resets whenever the pod restarts. increase(...[24h]) gives you the exact count inside your window while handling resets cleanly, far more readable than a tiny per-second rate().

Your new ServiceMonitor for Argo CD produces no target. First two checks?

Verify endpoints[].port matches the Service's exact port name (http-metrics, not metrics or 8082) and confirm your label selector matches the Service's real labels. Then check Prometheus's serviceMonitorSelector and the monitor's namespaceSelector.

How would you measure "request to fulfillment" for a self-service API?

Measure the delta between a custom resource's metadata.creationTimestamp and the lastTransitionTime of its Ready condition, export it as a metric (via kube-state-metrics CustomResourceStateMetrics or a small exporter), and track p50/p95 over time.

Someone proposes measuring developer productivity by lines of code. Reply in one sentence.

Measure outcomes, not raw volume: DORA delivery metrics paired with platform-effectiveness measures (fulfillment latency, adoption, developer satisfaction) capture real value delivery, whereas line counts are trivially gamed and reward bloat.

Name the five DORA metrics as the 2024 report defines them, and say which group each belongs to.

Throughput: change lead time, deployment frequency, and failed deployment recovery time. Stability: change failure rate and deployment rework rate (unplanned hotfixes for user-facing bugs, added in 2024). Operational reliability is tracked alongside them rather than as a sixth key, and the 2025 report moved from four clusters to team archetypes.

The white paper's "organizational efficiency" group: which three latencies does it name, and how would you get the first one out of this lab?

Request-to-fulfillment latency for a capability, time to build and deploy a brand-new service to production, and time for a new developer to submit their first change. Pull the first from metadata.creationTimestamp to the Ready condition's lastTransitionTime on our Crossplane XR or kro instance, exposed via CustomResourceStateMetrics.

Which platform-tool metric would you alert on to learn that the platform, not the workloads, is degrading, and what kind of SLO fits it?

Alert on control-plane SLIs: Argo CD sync success ratio (argocd_app_sync_total), Crossplane/kro reconcile error rate (controller_runtime_reconcile_total{result="error"}), or webhook admission latency. Give it a 30-day availability SLO (like 99.5% clean reconciles) guarded by section 4.2's multi-window burn-rate rules.
